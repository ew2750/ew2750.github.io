Analysis

Neural and behavioral data aren’t always clean, and they never are. I enjoy exploring different ways of analysis that can turn noisy data into robust scientific results.

Continuous-time deconvolutional regression

CDR

Guiding question

How do linguistic features influence responses over time when successive words arrive at irregular intervals?

Why this analysis?

CDR estimates the delayed and overlapping effects of timestamped predictors. It is useful for naturalistic language because a word’s influence can persist beyond its onset, rather than fitting neatly into fixed time bins.

Generalized additive models

GAM

Guiding question

When do neural responses diverge across linguistic features, conditions, or recorded brain regions?

Why this analysis?

GAMs model smooth, potentially nonlinear relationships without imposing a straight-line effect or a fixed response shape. They help characterize how intracortical activity varies over time and across task variables.

Principal component analysis

PCA

Guiding question

What dominant patterns organize the activity of many recorded neurons, and how do population states evolve during comprehension or speech planning?

Why this analysis?

PCA summarizes correlated activity in a smaller set of components, making high-dimensional population dynamics easier to visualize. It is an exploratory description of variance, not by itself evidence that a component encodes a particular linguistic feature.

Recoverable information in behavioral and neural patterns

Decoding

Guiding question

Can sentence identity, linguistic features, or task states be predicted from held-out behavioral or neural data?

Why this analysis?

Cross-validated decoding tests whether information is recoverable beyond the training sample. For sentence repetition, it helps distinguish increased sentence-specific structure from a general change in response magnitude; for neural recordings, it tests which features population activity can predict.

Generalized psychophysiological interactions

gPPI

Guiding question

Does coupling between language and speech–motor regions change as a sequence is cued, maintained, and executed?

Why this analysis?

gPPI models how the relationship between a seed region and other regions varies with task context, beyond their overall activity levels. It addresses condition-dependent functional coupling, not the direction of information flow or a causal connection.

Representational similarity analysis

RSA

Guiding question

Do differences among neural patterns reflect sequence identity, modality, task phase, or linguistic structure?

Why this analysis?

RSA compares the geometry of response patterns with predictions from task variables or computational models. It tests how conditions are organized relative to one another, rather than relying only on average activation or a single decoding score.
