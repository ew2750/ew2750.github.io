Hear my name

I study how the human brain turns a continuous stream of sensory inputs into what we experience as language.

I am a lab manager in the Department of Linguistics at Stanford University, where I work with Dr. Cory Shain. My research interests lie at the intersection of cognitive neuroscience, psycholinguistics, and computational modeling. My central question is how the human brain constructs and maintains linguistic representations from temporal unfolding of sensory inputs. This process is complex yet efficiently and fast enough to support our real-time language use. How?

My interests follow three connected directions. First, I study incremental language comprehension: how lexical, syntactic, and semantic information is encoded and integrated in real time, and what linguistic or contextual factors contribute to processing difficulty and neural effort. Second, I am drawn to the translational challenge of communication restoration for populations living with impared language abilities. I investigate neural mechanisms that could inform brain–computer interfaces, including the cortical organization of speech planning, sequence processing, and word decoding. Third, I am curious about bilingual neural plasticity, with particular interest in how Mandarin–English experience and proficiency reshape language representations and speech control.

Methodologically, I am interested in exploring time-resolved and spatially precise measures of brain activity with behavioral experiments and explicit computational models. I am preparing to pursue Ph.D. training beginning in Fall 2027, and I am especially interested in research environments that connect rigorous theories of language with naturalistic data and human neuroscience.
